How to use CMRSARathi · Criterion 5

Prepare Criterion 5 once, then audit it like an NBA peer team

Faculty Information, 100 marks: student-faculty ratio, faculty qualification, cadre, visiting faculty and retention. No AI is used: every mark comes from the NBA formulas.

The flow in 5 steps

  • 1
    Bring in the faculty list (Table B2.A)Faculty list › Import: an Excel/CSV export from the ERP, or add people one by one. Department and allied departments together.
  • 2
    Type the few numbers that are not in the listNumbers (5.1–5.5): student strength S and DS from the ERP admissions data, and the pre-qualifier facts. Everything else is counted from the list.
  • 3
    Upload the proofsOn each person's page: the appointment letter and the highest degree certificate (PDF or phone photo). They are read with free OCR and checked at once.
  • 4
    Add visiting faculty (5.4)Items & documents › 5.4: fill Table 5.4.1 (year, name, course, hours). The yearly hours are added up for you.
  • 5
    SAR documentThe website drafts the text and fills the NBA tables from the data. Members polish their sections like in Google Docs; everyone sees changes live.
  • 6
    Run the audit and fixMarks for 5.1–5.5 with the working, marks if only checked proofs count, the faculty whose proofs to fix, pre-qualifiers and likely peer-team questions.

Good to know

  • Years: Criterion 5 counts CAY, CAYm1 and CAYm2 (5.4 counts CAYm1 to CAYm3). A person counts in a year if they were on the rolls for at least 6 months of it.
  • Promotions and Ph.D.: designation and degree are taken as on 1 January of each year, using the date of designation and the Ph.D. award date in the list.
  • Two sets of marks: "as claimed" counts the whole list; "only checked proofs" counts only people whose appointment letter (and degree certificate for Ph.D./M.Tech) matched. The gap is what a strict peer team could cut.
  • Formulas follow the NBA SAR format and Evaluation Guidelines (Tier I, 2026) and are tested against NBA's own worked examples. The NBA peer team takes the final decision.
